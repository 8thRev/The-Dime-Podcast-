Cannabis operators can hit their KPI and still hurt the business.

**That is not a failure of effort. It is a failure of what the effort was pointed at.**

An immature industry can mistake activity for performance.

Units produced. Orders shipped. Inventory turned. Every number moves, every dashboard is green, and every team can show proof that it did its job.

Each function is rewarded for something different.

Operations is rewarded for throughput.

Finance wants margin.

Sales wants inventory.

Leadership wants predictable forecasting.

Each of those is a reasonable thing to want. The trouble starts when the incentives don't connect.

**Everyone can do their job correctly and the business can still get the wrong result.**

That is what makes it hard to catch. Nobody missed a target. Nobody broke a process. The report shows a team that delivered.

The cost shows up somewhere else, later, in a place the metric was never built to see.

> **The operator's job is to maximize the metric. Leadership's job is to make sure they picked the right one.**

When a metric hurts the business, the operator is usually not the one who got it wrong. They did exactly what they were asked to do, and they did it well.

The question for leadership is not whether the team hit the number.

**It is whether the number is still worth hitting.**
