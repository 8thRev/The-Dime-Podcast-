## The short version

Cannabis real estate runs on location, profitability, and structural workarounds for federal illegality. Lenders and REITs underwrite on debt service coverage and property-level cash flow, not on hype about reform, as Joe Lustberg of Upwise Capital explains in detail [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss). Triple net leases and sale-leasebacks exist because banks won't lend like they would to any other industry, per Anthony Coniglio of NewLake Capital Partners [Capital Injections, REITs, Location Opportunities](/episodes/anthony-coniglio-capital-injections-reits-location-opportunities) and Rob Sechrist of Polaris Capital Group [The Future of the Industry](/episodes/the-future-of-the-industry-state-packs-market-dynamics-safe-banking-capitol-hill-updates-ft-rob-sechrist). Zoning and local permitting fights can tie up a project for years even after a license is won, as both Joe Lustberg and dispensary owner Markel Bababekov describe from personal experience [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss), [First-time Dispensary owner details his experience with the NY CAURD Program](/episodes/first-time-dispensary-owner-details-his-experience-with-the-ny-caurd-program-ft-markel-bababekov). Across guests, 280E repeal, not SAFE Banking, is repeatedly named as the reform that would actually move operator economics.

## Where guests agree

Multiple guests converge on the same core point: profitability, not federal reform, drives access to capital and real estate. Joe Lustberg says lenders require demonstrated profitability before extending debt, and that rescheduling or SAFE Banking won't meaningfully move rates in the near term [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss). Rob Sechrist makes nearly the identical case from the lending side, noting 684 banks already accept cannabis deposits, so SAFE Banking wouldn't solve the industry's real cash problem, which he says is the inability to process credit cards on federal networks [The Future of the Industry](/episodes/the-future-of-the-industry-state-packs-market-dynamics-safe-banking-capitol-hill-updates-ft-rob-sechrist).

Both Lustberg and Sechrist separately name 280E repeal as the more consequential federal lever. Lustberg frames it as the trigger that would make many EBITDA-positive operators suddenly qualify for better loan terms [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss). Sechrist puts a number on it, estimating a 20-40% net income uplift for nearly every operator [The Future of the Industry](/episodes/the-future-of-the-industry-state-packs-market-dynamics-safe-banking-capitol-hill-updates-ft-rob-sechrist). Anthony Coniglio agrees from the REIT side, saying 280E elimination would improve the credit quality of NewLake's entire tenant portfolio overnight [Capital Injections, REITs, Location Opportunities](/episodes/anthony-coniglio-capital-injections-reits-location-opportunities).

Location and alternative-use value also come up repeatedly as the top underwriting factor. Lustberg says a property with no viable non-cannabis use is much harder to finance than one near a city center [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss). Coniglio says NewLake favors limited-license states like Illinois, Pennsylvania, and Ohio because constrained competition supports more reliable rent payments [Capital Injections, REITs, Location Opportunities](/episodes/anthony-coniglio-capital-injections-reits-location-opportunities), a preference Lustberg echoes when naming Missouri, Kentucky, Arkansas, Ohio, and New Jersey as currently attractive lending markets [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss).

Local zoning and permitting risk is another shared theme, though from different vantage points. David Fettner of Grow America Builders says signing a lease before an experienced builder evaluates a site is the biggest mistake operators make, since a building may not support the intended canopy or power needs [Secrets Behind Failed Cannabis Builds](/episodes/secrets-behind-failed-cannabis-builds-where-every-team-needs-to-look-now-ft-david-fettner). Emily Sisneros of Canna Business Services describes municipalities opting out of cannabis programs preemptively, often without understanding what opting in would require [Kentucky's Hot Medical Market and Winning Cannabis Licenses](/episodes/emily-sisneros-kentuckys-hot-medical-market-and-winning-cannabis-licenses). Markel Bababekov lived this directly in New York, where a court injunction froze his CAURD license communication with the state while he continued an expensive buildout [First-time Dispensary owner details his experience with the NY CAURD Program](/episodes/first-time-dispensary-owner-details-his-experience-with-the-ny-caurd-program-ft-markel-bababekov), and Joe Lustberg spent over two years in a rezoning and litigation battle with the Town of Southampton, New York, over his own dispensary site [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss).

## Where guests disagree

The sources do not show guests arguing opposite positions on the same real estate question. Instead they emphasize different risks within a shared framework. Rob Sechrist warns that sale-leasebacks can misalign operator incentives, lock operators into rising rent escalators, and create systemic write-down risk if interstate commerce ever removes state-level market protections [The Future of the Industry](/episodes/the-future-of-the-industry-state-packs-market-dynamics-safe-banking-capitol-hill-updates-ft-rob-sechrist). Anthony Coniglio, whose company NewLake structures exactly these deals, presents sale-leasebacks as a mainstream, low-risk corporate finance tool used broadly outside cannabis, one that frees operator capital for growth [Capital Injections, REITs, Location Opportunities](/episodes/anthony-coniglio-capital-injections-reits-location-opportunities). Neither guest directly rebuts the other since they appear in different episodes months apart, but the open question for operators is real: is a sale-leaseback a smart capital tool or a long-term rent trap if state protections ever erode. The transcripts don't resolve it.

## How the view has changed

The earliest episode in this set, Rob Sechrist in August 2023, frames cannabis real estate finance as still maturing, with each state acting as its own distinct market and SAFE Banking oversold as a fix for problems banking access had mostly already solved [The Future of the Industry](/episodes/the-future-of-the-industry-state-packs-market-dynamics-safe-banking-capitol-hill-updates-ft-rob-sechrist). By early 2024, Anthony Coniglio's NewLake was actively expanding triple-net-lease and sale-leaseback deals in limited-license states, describing a steady institutional REIT strategy [Capital Injections, REITs, Location Opportunities](/episodes/anthony-coniglio-capital-injections-reits-location-opportunities), while Emily Sisneros in mid 2024 was tracking a wave of new limited-license markets, like Kentucky, opening up licensing rounds [Kentucky's Hot Medical Market and Winning Cannabis Licenses](/episodes/emily-sisneros-kentuckys-hot-medical-market-and-winning-cannabis-licenses). By mid to late 2025, the construction and build-out conversation, per David Fettner, had shifted toward technical execution risk, AI-assisted plant monitoring, and climate-specific engineering, rather than licensing access [Secrets Behind Failed Cannabis Builds](/episodes/secrets-behind-failed-cannabis-builds-where-every-team-needs-to-look-now-ft-david-fettner). By May 2026, Joe Lustberg describes overbuilt, over-financed facilities being foreclosed on or sold at a discount as markets compress, a distress dynamic not present in the earlier episodes [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss). Across this span, the consistent thread is that 280E repeal, not banking reform, has remained the reform guests say would matter most, named by Sechrist in 2023, Coniglio in 2024, and Lustberg in 2026.

## What it means for operators

Underwrite to profitability and location, not to anticipated federal reform. Joe Lustberg's numbers, roughly 65% loan-to-cost from lenders with operators covering the remainder plus a burn capital cushion, and debt service coverage requirements around 1.25 to 1.3x, are a useful baseline for what real lenders expect today [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss). Bring a design-build partner in before signing a lease, per David Fettner, to avoid the mismatched-building and underspecified-budget mistakes that sink projects after the fact [Secrets Behind Failed Cannabis Builds](/episodes/secrets-behind-failed-cannabis-builds-where-every-team-needs-to-look-now-ft-david-fettner). Expect zoning and permitting risk to persist regardless of state maturity, as both Markel Bababekov and Joe Lustberg experienced directly [First-time Dispensary owner details his experience with the NY CAURD Program](/episodes/first-time-dispensary-owner-details-his-experience-with-the-ny-caurd-program-ft-markel-bababekov), [What Cannabis Lenders See That Operators Miss](/episodes/joe-lustberg-what-cannabis-lenders-see-that-operators-miss). Weigh sale-leaseback structures carefully against Rob Sechrist's warning on long-term rent escalators before treating them as free capital [The Future of the Industry](/episodes/the-future-of-the-industry-state-packs-market-dynamics-safe-banking-capitol-hill-updates-ft-rob-sechrist).
