The honest answer is that credit card processing for cannabis is not a product category, it's a race against discovery. Visa and Mastercard don't carve out exceptions for state-legal cannabis. They follow federal law, and federal law says cannabis is illegal, so neither network allows its rails to be used for a cannabis purchase, full stop.

## Every workaround is a misrepresentation waiting to be caught

Sundie Seefried, CEO of Safe Harbor Financial, laid out exactly how the common workarounds function: a broker sets up a card terminal, categorizes the business as something it isn't (a flower shop, a gift shop), and runs transactions under that false merchant category code. As she put it, "that's really lying to the financial system, misrepresenting it." Visa and Mastercard are left holding risk they didn't agree to, and their response is to find the misrepresentation and blacklist it. Seefried was clear that SAFE Banking wouldn't change this dynamic on its own, since the card networks' refusal isn't a banking compliance problem, it's a categorization problem that may require deregulation to fix. She noted debit cards for non-cannabis spending, like paying a budtender or funding a Home Depot run, are a different story and generally tolerated. ## Even compliant-looking solutions get pulled without warning

Aubrey Amatelli, founder of PayRio, built a card processing product specifically to follow every Visa and Mastercard rule: proper funds flow, correct merchant category code, no tricks. She assumed that made it bulletproof. It didn't. As she described it, "Visa, Mastercard, billion-dollar companies, they can come in and squash whatever they want to squash, even if we can fight and say, but look, we're doing it the right way." When a network pulls support, the shutoff happens immediately through the sponsoring bank, with no appeals process, and funds already in flight can get frozen for up to 180 days. That's the real cost structure operators need to understand before they lean on any single credit processing solution. See [The Legal Market Was Never Built for Payments](/episodes/the-legal-market-was-never-built-for-payments-why-cannabis-still-breaks-the-rules-ft-aubrey-amatelli).

## What this means for a dispensary owner

Don't build your point-of-sale strategy around a single credit workaround surviving indefinitely. Amatelli's response to this reality was redundancy: multiple processors, multiple bank sponsors, multiple rails feeding the same terminal, so when one gets cut off another activates without the register going dark. If you're running a single cashless ATM or a single wallet-app workaround with no backup, you are one network audit away from a dead terminal and frozen funds. Plan for the shutdown before it happens, not after.
