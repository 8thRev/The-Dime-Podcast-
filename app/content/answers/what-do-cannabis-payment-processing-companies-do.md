Start with what these companies are not. They are not Stripe for weed. A traditional payment processor builds its own rails, owns the relationship with the card network, and sells a single clean product. Cannabis payment companies can't do that, because Visa and Mastercard won't let cannabis transactions run directly on their rails as long as cannabis is federally illegal. So the companies that call themselves cannabis payment processors are really doing something else: they are sourcing other people's technology, stitching it into a terminal, and managing the risk that any one piece of it gets pulled without warning.

## Redundancy is the actual product

Aubrey Amatelli, founder of PayRio, learned this the hard way. She came from JPMorgan believing that a compliant solution, built on the correct merchant category code and following Visa and Mastercard's rules, would be bulletproof. It wasn't. Mastercard paused her product to question how funds were flowing, and years later, by Amatelli's account, Mastercard shut down one of the larger processors in the industry and left over 5,000 dispensaries without payments for weeks. Her response was to build terminals with multiple processors and bank sponsors running underneath a single device, so that if one rail gets shut off, another activates automatically and the dispensary never notices. That redundancy, not any single piece of technology, is what a cannabis payment company is actually selling. ## These companies don't build their own tech, and that's a real cost

Amatelli says PayRio doesn't develop products internally. It finds tech companies, partners with them, and tailors what they've built for cannabis before bringing it to market. That means every new feature, whether it's a QuickBooks integration or a crypto on-ramp, depends on an outside partner holding up their end. Amatelli put a number on the downside: if a partnership falls apart, a company like hers can be out six figures or more. That's the quiet cost of not owning your own stack, and it's a cost every cannabis payment company absorbs so operators don't have to think about it.

## What this means for an operator evaluating a processor

Don't evaluate a cannabis payment company on whether it claims to be compliant. Amatelli's own experience shows that following Visa and Mastercard's rules to the letter didn't stop a shutdown. Evaluate it on redundancy: how many processors and bank sponsors are actually running behind the terminal you'd be using, and what happens operationally the moment one gets cut. A company with one rail is a company one phone call from Mastercard away from leaving you without payments for weeks. A company with three or four rails built into the same terminal is the one that keeps your register running through the next shutdown, because there will be a next one as long as cannabis stays federally illegal.
