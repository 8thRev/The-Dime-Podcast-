There is no version of dispensary payment processing that works like a normal retail business, and anyone telling you otherwise hasn't been shut down yet.

Aubrey Amatelli, founder of PayRio, built a fully compliant cannabis payment solution and still got pulled by Mastercard. Not because she broke a rule. Because Visa and Mastercard treat federal legality as the actual test, and cannabis fails it regardless of MCC codes or state licensing. As she put it on [The Legal Market Was Never Built for Payments](/episodes/the-legal-market-was-never-built-for-payments-why-cannabis-still-breaks-the-rules-ft-aubrey-amatelli), "Visa, Mastercard, billion-dollar companies, they can come in and squash whatever they want to squash, even if we can fight and say, 'But look, we're doing it the right way.'"

## A shutdown is instant and funds can freeze for 180 days

When a card network decides to pull support, it goes straight to the sponsoring bank and orders the rail cut off. There's no appeals process. Bryan Fields summed up the experience on the show: "There's no explanation, there's no real appeals process, it's just, 'Sorry, move on.'" Worse, funds already in flight can get frozen at the bank for up to 180 days while the bank protects itself against chargebacks and fraud. For a cash-heavy business already living on thin margins, that's not a technical inconvenience. That's a liquidity crisis.

## Redundancy is the actual product operators are buying

The practical answer to "how does dispensary payment processing work" is: it works because processors build in backups. PayRio stacks multiple processors, multiple bank sponsors, and multiple rails into a single terminal, so when one gets shut off, another activates with no interruption at the register. Amatelli calls this the first lesson of the business: "the importance of redundancy in payments, never having all your eggs in one basket." Consumer card transactions typically run through a KYC layer too, an ID photo plus facial recognition, which exists specifically to protect the dispensary from the fraud and chargeback exposure that got the whole industry locked out of standard rails in the first place.

## Cash is shrinking but it isn't gone, and rescheduling won't finish the job

Card acceptance has clearly changed unit economics. Moving a dispensary from cash to debit lifts average ticket size by more than 25%, and layering credit on top adds another 20%. Cash-only dispensaries have dropped to roughly 10 to 15% of new onboards. But don't expect rescheduling to close the gap. Amatelli is direct that Visa and Mastercard require full federal legalization, not a schedule change, before they'll treat cannabis like any other retail category. On the operations side, this is also why point-of-sale systems for dispensaries look nothing like a standard retail stack. Anne Forkutza of Dutchie noted on [All-in-One Dispensary Solution](/episodes/all-in-one-dispensary-solution-ft-anne-forkutza) that SAFE Banking would reduce reliance on cash-only operations, which pose real security risk on their own.

If you're picking a payment provider, the question isn't whether they follow Visa and Mastercard rules. Everyone claims that. The question is what happens the day the network shuts you off anyway, and whether your provider has a second and third rail already live.
