CBD didn't take center stage at the EO press conference by accident.

It's the lowest-friction entry point for federal oversight in cannabis. No intoxication. Simple explanations. Broad public acceptance.

That's the tell.

Change isn't hitting this industry all at once. It's entering through a different door first.

As everyone takes a breath after a historic day, one thing is clear:

> **Change is here.**

And change doesn't mean good or bad. Change means *different*.

Different brings opportunity.

Different brings new challenges.

Different brings new entrants.

Different brings new capital and new partners.

But opportunity is the one that matters most.

CBD is a gateway cannabinoid for the newly informed. It's easy to explain. Easy to accept. "You won't get high" is a clean, digestible on-ramp.

CBD has accepted medical benefits. That matters when you're introducing regulation, research, and standards to a broader audience.

Expanded research — both the benefits and the risks — is a net positive. The implications for veterans alone are massive and overdue.

What really matters next, though, is what follows CBD.

cGMP.

**The FDA will not invent a new playbook for cannabis.** It will apply the one it already knows from food, pharma, and supplements. And it will expect companies in this space to invest in the resources, tools, and systems required to comply.

That's where the conversation shifts from headlines to infrastructure.

Expect these terms to move from niche jargon to standard language in boardrooms and emergency planning meetings:

> Process Control, Process Stability, Process Analytical Technology

As Zach said on The Dime Podcast:

> "Process analytical technology is just starting to see its day in cannabis, but that's table stakes in pharma because it's too expensive to make an error."

That's the deeper shift most people are missing.

With a single signature and a short press conference, consistency, traceability, and real-time visibility move from "nice to have" to non-negotiable. This is where [platforms like Newton](https://www.newton-insights.com/) stop being optional tools and start becoming operational infrastructure — a way to see variability as it happens, minimize drift, and defend decisions before they become problems.

Yesterday was historic.

What matters now is what companies do *after* the headline.

Be proactive. Be ready. Do the work now.
