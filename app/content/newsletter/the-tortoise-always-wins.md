Momentum matters.

When challenges stack up and setbacks hit, it's easy to get lost in the short term.

Most companies sprinted toward growth — burning capital, chasing scale, and calling it strategy. The few that didn't? The disciplined ones? They looked slow. Cautious. Even boring.

But the tortoise always wins.

Those early decisions — the ones that seemed conservative, are now proving to be a masterclass in timing, experience, and restraint. What once looked like missed opportunities are now strategic blessings.

Jared Maloof of Standard Wellness saw this years ago — a disciplined approach that looked slow then, but now stands out as foresight.

Because as the debt wall closes in, the market will reward one thing: **Prudent capital allocators** — operators who deliver predictable, stable returns.

Guesswork and volatility are for the "growth at all costs" crowd — and they're the ones paying for it now.

Strategic location choices, disciplined expansion, and markets like Utah built on profitability create stable, cash-flowing operations. That stability gives you the luxury of being proactive — not reactive — when everyone else is scrambling.

Early setbacks? They're blessings in disguise. They force you to adapt, find discipline, and see the game differently. They sharpen your risk tolerance and shape your long-term strategy.

In cannabis, speed kills. Everyone wants to scale fast — until they realize the smartest players are moving slow on purpose.
