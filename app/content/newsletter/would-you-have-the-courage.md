**I found myself thinking — would I have the courage and the patience to make that decision like Chris Ball?**

Building something real takes *always* longer than you plan.

It costs more — financially, physically, and emotionally.

And in hindsight, once you've reached the summit, most founders admit: if they knew what it would take, they might not have started.

That's the courage it takes to build a brand — one with your family's name on it. Putting your name on something means there's nowhere to hide when things go wrong, and no shortcuts when things get hard. It requires tough decisions, uncomfortable patience, and the willingness to take the long road when the easy one looks tempting.

During our conversation with **Chris Ball**, I kept coming back to that same theme — hard decisions that went against the grain.

**Daniel LaRusso** was one of them.

A nod to his movie-buff personality, the name sent a subtle but direct message to his audience about grit, discipline, and staying true to the craft.

These decisions are easy to reflect on now, but the journey was anything but simple. There were years of setbacks, restarts, and moments of faith when the payoff was still invisible.

That unwavering mindset runs through everything at **Ball Family Farms** — and it's contagious. It's what drew in like-minded people like **Hoodbaby**, who helped bring Ball Family Farms their first trophy. That win didn't just validate the brand; it opened the door for the **Rare Breed Mentorship Program**, designed to teach others.

Because sometimes, building something real isn't about knowing it will work. It's about trusting that if you keep showing up, the work will take care of you.
