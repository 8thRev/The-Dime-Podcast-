Is all debt bad? Or is *bad* debt bad?

This changed things for me.

I always thought debt was bad. But what if only bad debt is bad — and some debt is not only good, but a lever to fuel growth?

Debt in cannabis is synonymous with "danger."

And honestly — it makes sense. Teams carry massive debt loads.

Debt obligations can absolutely crush a business. We've seen it happen over and over.

But here's the part most teams never hear:

**Debt isn't the problem. The terms are. The structure is. The timing is.** And the biggest misconception of all — how teams *think* about debt in the first place.

Because on the flip side…

**When structured correctly, debt becomes a growth tool.** A force multiplier. A way to move faster, not slower.

If I can use Adam Stettner's FundCanna capital to fuel growth instead of draining my own balance sheet, wouldn't that be better?

Most teams only experience debt through predatory arrangements or panic borrowing — high interest, no flexibility, backed into a corner. No strategy. No leverage. Just survival.

So the conclusion becomes: **Debt = danger.**

But that's not the reality in any mature industry.

Every major sector — manufacturing, CPG, logistics, tech — scales on borrowed capital. Not because they're desperate, but because it accelerates growth when used correctly.

**We don't have to do it alone.**

Most teams think debt is dangerous. What if the real problem is how we think about it?

Because if your margins beat your cost of capital, you're not "borrowing to stay alive." You're borrowing to outrun everyone else.

The math works — if the team understands where, why, and how to deploy it.

Growth requires stability — and this is where tools like Newton matter more than people realize.

You cannot make smart financial decisions if you can't trust your own process.

You can't forecast cash flow if you can't predict your output. And you can't scale a brand when your product swings from batch to batch.

[**Newton gives extraction teams the visibility and consistency needed to make debt work for them, not against them — predictable inputs, predictable outputs, predictable decisions.**](https://www.newton-insights.com/)

Stability isn't a luxury. It's what allows teams to use capital intelligently instead of reactively.

So if debt feels scary, that's understandable. But the fear usually comes from past experiences, bad structures, or operating without the stability required to use financing as a tool.

**Debt isn't the enemy — misalignment is. And when used correctly, it's the single fastest way to scale.**

This week we sit down with Adam Stettner to break down the playbook behind smart leverage, common mistakes, and the exact moment when debt goes from dangerous to powerful.
