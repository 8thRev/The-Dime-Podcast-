**If you think consumers will never choose cannabis by effect, you're already behind.**

Effects-based products seem impossible for most cannabis brands — but only for one reason. Most operators don't have in-house analytics. That's the miss.

Process control is the foundation for delivering a consistent experience. It's the only way it happens. If you want consistency in the final product, you need consistency at every single step of the production process.

Otherwise you're guessing. And when your starting material is a plant — with natural variation, shifting composition, and uncontrollable inputs — expecting a consistent formulation at the end is not realistic.

I hear you: "Bryan… *money, time, resources. Cash is tight. How do we justify it?"*

That investment is the brand. It's exactly why a consumer chooses one product over another. It's why I choose LEVEL — the products are consistent. I know I won't fall asleep on the couch when I take Boost.

That is how Chris Emerson approaches it — testing at every step so that by the time it hits a third-party lab, it's a formality, not a question of consistency or quality.

That's an expectation, not a luxury.

And right now, the consumer experience is this: "I thought sativa was energizing, but it put me down." "It said sleepy, but I wasn't sleepy." "These gummies didn't do anything."

That gap — between expectation and experience — is exactly why consumers don't return to certain brands.

As the category matures and consumers sift through endless choices, the brands delivering predictable effects will win. The ones winging it on formulation will fall off a cliff.

If you think effect-based products will never matter, skip the analytics. If you believe consumers will choose cannabis the same way they choose supplements, nootropics, wellness products, or beverages — based on effect — then formulation control is not optional.
