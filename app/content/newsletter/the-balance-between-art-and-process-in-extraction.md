Extraction is not purely art. And it is not purely rigid SOP.

It is a delicate balance between the intended finished product, the limitations of the system, the variability of the material, and the foresight to let operators maneuver between those constraints while still operating efficiently.

> **That balance is rare.**

Most teams lean too far in one direction.

Over-standardize and quality suffers. Over-index on intuition and repeatability disappears.

The teams that win learn how to hold both at the same time.

This level of nuance is hard. The pace is fast. The margin for error is small.

Precision is required at every step, even when conditions change. Those details matter. Tracking them is what allows teams to produce a premium, consistent end product instead of hoping the next run looks like the last one.

That discipline is exactly how Nimbus grew into a brand known for consistency and quality.

But getting there was not linear.

Like most things in cannabis, it was a series of steps forward and steps back. Decisions reinforced by data. Adjustments made based on what actually happened, not what was expected. Data used to inform judgment, not stored for a rainy day.

This is where [Newton thrives](https://www.newton-insights.com/).

Not as another layer of software, but as operational infrastructure. A way to surface variability, reinforce decision making, and give operators visibility without stripping them of control.

As extraction operations grow, teams face a real tension. Standardize too early and you lose quality. Rely too heavily on intuition and you lose repeatability.

This episode unpacks how experienced operators navigate that balance.

Brian Adams walks through how extraction decisions evolve with scale, why SOPs should guide rather than replace judgment, and how quality is preserved as processes mature.

This is a practical conversation about understanding variability, respecting the craft, and building systems that support operators instead of overriding them.
